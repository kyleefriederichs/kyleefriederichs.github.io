About Me

I am a PhD student in Computer Engineering researching how sociotemporal human behaviors can serve as persistent and secure indicators of identity in computational systems. My work focuses on modeling micro-temporal and expressive behavioral patterns—such as conversational rhythm, reaction timing, gesture dynamics, and interaction pacing—to develop behavioral signatures that remain stable across sessions, contexts, and embodiments. This research supports next-generation authentication and identity frameworks that operate beyond appearance, enabling trustworthy, avatar-agnostic identity across digital and immersive environments. My background in computer engineering and human-computer interaction informs this work, particularly as it relates to behavioral biometrics, signal processing, and secure system design. I also draw on prior experience with VR/XR environments to understand how identity is expressed through embodied interaction. Outside of research, I enjoy 3D modeling, game development, and digital art, often blending creative and technical practices across my projects.
