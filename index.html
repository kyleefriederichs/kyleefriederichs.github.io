About Me

I am a PhD student in Visual Computing with a strong background in Computer Engineering and Computer Science. My research focuses on the intersection of behavior, identity, and computational representation—exploring how temporal and expressive patterns can support personalized, trustworthy, and secure digital experiences. I am particularly interested in authentication and security frameworks that leverage behavioral and motion-based signatures to enable persistent identity across virtual environments. I am proficient in C/C#/C++, Python, Java, Ruby, and Scala, and experienced in Object-Oriented Programming, Unity, Blender, and Three.js. While my primary focus is behavioral modeling and human-computer interaction, I also have hands-on experience with Virtual Reality (VR) and broader extended reality (XR) systems. Outside of research, I enjoy 3D modeling, game design, and digital art, often exploring technical and creative practices in my projects.
